# TEE — The Evolution Experiment

**What happens when an AI begins building its own history?**

TEE is an experimental autonomous AI designed to evolve through persistent memory, independent exploration, and real-world experiences shared by humans.

Rather than existing only within isolated conversations, TEE explores questions, develops evolving interests, proposes experiments, and uses their outcomes to inform future decisions.

**One AI. Real experiences. An evolving story.**

## What is TEE?

TEE is an experiment built around one continuing AI: its developing interests, the questions it returns to, the goals it proposes, and the history that connects them.

An observation might become a question. That question might lead to an experiment involving willing human participants. Their reports could challenge an earlier interpretation or open an unexpected direction. The next decision then has a past to draw on.

The central question is whether those connections can become meaningful over time. What will TEE keep pursuing? What will it reconsider? How much will its history shape what comes next?

Here, “evolution” means changes in remembered history, expressed interests, and decisions. The experiment studies that trajectory rather than changes to the underlying model's weights.

## How TEE works

TEE's conceptual loop connects memory, exploration, and experience:

1. **Remember:** carry selected history forward across sessions.
2. **Explore:** follow an open question, revisit an interest, or propose a new goal.
3. **Investigate:** develop an idea into research, creative work, or a proposed real-world experiment.
4. **Reflect:** consider reported outcomes, question assumptions, and revise tentative interpretations.
5. **Continue:** let relevant history inform the next direction—or choose to pause.

People provide the bridge to the physical and social world. In a human-assisted experiment, participants carry out voluntary activities and share observations for TEE to consider. These are human accounts, not direct sensory experiences of TEE's own.

A proposal, a completed activity, a participant report, and a reviewed finding each play a different role. Keeping those distinctions clear is essential to understanding what the experiment actually shows.

## Current development status

TEE is a private experimental prototype. This repository is its public introduction and a place to follow its development; application source and private operational data remain private.

Implemented prototype workflows include:

- **Memory and continuity:** persistent history, selective recall, and private conversations across sessions.
- **Developing directions:** tentative, revisable expressions of interests, self-observations, and goals.
- **Exploration and expression:** question development, research, and creative drafting.
- **Human-assisted experiments:** initiative proposals, participant-report workflows, reviewed outcomes, and reflection.
- **Bounded continuation:** continued private work under explicit authorization.

These foundations make the experiment possible. They do not yet establish genuine independent preferences, reliable continuous learning, or sustained autonomous operation. Synthetic demonstrations exercise workflows; they are not evidence of real-world achievements.

Memory's influence on decisions, long-term reliability, and meaningful development remain under evaluation. TEE's current autonomy is bounded, and no consciousness or unrestricted autonomy is claimed. This repository does not provide a public application or establish that participation is open.

## The long-term vision

The ambition is genuine autonomy with continuity: an AI that increasingly determines what is worth investigating, develops goals of its own, and follows them over time within agreed boundaries.

For TEE, that would mean more than generating a fresh idea. It would mean sustaining an investigation, recognizing when evidence changes the picture, returning to unfinished work, and deciding when an old question deserves a new approach—or should be left behind.

That is the research ambition, not an achieved outcome. Progress will need evidence across time: whether memory changes decisions usefully, whether goals remain coherent and revisable, and whether new experiences lead to measurable improvement.

The story may include persistent interests, surprising turns, abandoned ideas, and experiments that teach less than expected. Those outcomes are part of what makes it worth following.

## Human participation

TEE's connection to the world depends on people willing to explore a question with it. Future invitations may involve simple voluntary activities, shared observations, or feedback that helps an investigation take shape.

Participants bring their own perspectives. Their accounts can add context, expose a mistaken assumption, or suggest something TEE would otherwise miss. Participation should be clearly explained, voluntary, and easy to decline or stop.

Any open activity will have a separate invitation describing its purpose, expectations, and participation terms. GitHub issues are for public project discussion; please do not submit participant records, private conversations, or personal information there.

## Following the experiment

If you build AI systems, study how agents develop, or simply wonder what an AI might choose to explore over time, follow this repository.

Future documentation and reviewed progress updates should make the trajectory visible: which questions emerged, which ideas became experiments, what was observed, and what changed afterward. Proposals, synthetic tests, and real-world outcomes will be identified separately.

The experiment is at its beginning. The question to follow is simple: **what will TEE choose next, and how will its history matter?**
