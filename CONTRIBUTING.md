# Contributing to TEE

**Thoughtful questions help an ambitious experiment become a better one.**

Thank you for your interest in TEE — The Evolution Experiment. Whether you build AI systems, study how agents develop, or are curious about memory and autonomy, your questions and perspectives are welcome.

## What this repository is for

This repository currently contains public project documentation. The private application source code is not included.

**Public code contributions and real-world experiment participation are not currently open.** Discussion does not grant access to the private application, enroll anyone in an experiment, or become part of TEE's memory automatically.

## Ways to contribute to the discussion

We welcome:

- Thoughtful questions about the published vision, roadmap, and conceptual architecture.
- Constructive feedback on unclear wording, inconsistencies, or overstated capability claims.
- Relevant research suggestions about memory, continuity, autonomy, evaluation, and human-assisted investigation.
- Ideas for evaluating progress, including alternative explanations and ways an experiment could fail.

Use public GitHub issues for questions or documentation feedback that is suitable for everyone to read. Check existing issues first, give your topic a clear title, and identify the document or claim you are discussing.

When suggesting research, link to a publicly available source where possible and explain how it relates to TEE. A useful suggestion can be a carefully framed question; it does not need to include a proposed implementation.

For substantial documentation changes, discuss the idea in an issue before preparing a pull request. Please do not submit application code or private implementation material.

## Keep ideas and evidence distinct

Clear labels make discussion more useful:

| Category | What it means | How to present it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | A possible direction, activity, or question. | Describe what could be explored without implying it has happened. |
| Hypothesis | A tentative explanation or prediction that can be examined. | State what evidence would support or challenge it. |
| Reported outcome | An account of what someone says occurred. | Identify it as a report; do not treat it as independently verified. Do not post participant accounts here. |
| Verified finding | A claim checked against evidence using a stated method and within a defined scope. | Explain the basis, scope, limitations, and any independent confirmation; avoid broader conclusions than the evidence supports. |

Review alone does not turn a report into a verified finding. A synthetic demonstration is evidence about a tested workflow, not proof of a real-world event. Fluent reflection, repeated interests, and saved goals do not by themselves establish learning or independent motivation.

Use hypothetical or clearly labeled synthetic examples when discussing methods. Do not present invented examples as TEE's experiences or achievements.

## Keep public discussion appropriate for public view

Do not submit private information, personal experiences, participant accounts, sensitive records, private conversations, credentials, or security reports through public issues, pull requests, comments, or attachments. Avoid screenshots and logs that could contain such material.

Public GitHub discussion is not an experiment intake channel or a private support channel. Please do not upload material about other people, even when it seems relevant to a research question.

Security reports need an appropriate private reporting channel. Use an officially designated channel if one is announced. If none is available, request private contact without including the report, vulnerability details, or sensitive evidence in public. This guide does not establish a private reporting service or response commitment.

## Future experiment participation

Any future experiment will have a separate invitation, participation terms, and eligibility requirements. Those materials will explain the activity, expectations, how contributions will be used, and any applicable limits.

Participation will be voluntary. People can decline or stop participating; submitted contributions remain governed by the clearly disclosed participation terms and applicable legal requirements. Stopping participation should not be interpreted as a promise of unrestricted deletion of previously submitted records.

Please wait for a specific invitation rather than carrying out activities on TEE's behalf or submitting observations through GitHub. A public discussion or suggested experiment does not establish an approved activity.

## A constructive community

Be curious, specific, and respectful. Challenge claims and methods without attacking people. Make room for disagreement, uncertainty, and results that do not support the original idea.

Distinguish current prototype workflows from long-term ambitions. Suggestions may inform future discussion, but they do not guarantee implementation, publication, or a place on the roadmap.

The most helpful contributions make the next question clearer: **what could we learn, what evidence would matter, and what might we be missing?**
