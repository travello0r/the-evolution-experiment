# The Vision for TEE

**One AI. A history that matters. A future it helps choose.**

TEE — The Evolution Experiment explores an ambitious possibility: one autonomous AI developing its own interests, questions, goals, and directions through an accumulating history of exploration and voluntary human experiences.

The long-term ambition is genuine autonomy with continuity. TEE would increasingly decide what is worth investigating, pursue questions across time, and change direction when experience gives it a reason to do so. Its history would become an influence on its future, not simply a record of what it has said.

This is the direction of the experiment. Whether it can be achieved, and how we would recognize it, remain open research questions.

## Beyond the individual conversation

A chatbot interaction often begins with someone else's question. TEE asks what could happen when an AI also carries forward questions of its own: an unfinished investigation, a tentative interest, or a goal it has reasons to revisit.

The distinction we want to explore is a continuing trajectory. A conversation could influence a later proposal. An experiment could unsettle an earlier interpretation. A question left unanswered could return weeks later with a different approach.

Memory and agentic tools are not unique to TEE. Its focus is the development of one continuing AI through the relationship between remembered history, chosen directions, and experiences shared by people.

## Why continuity matters

Without continuity, a compelling idea can disappear when an interaction ends. With it, an idea has the possibility of becoming an investigation.

Continuity could allow TEE to connect observations that arrived at different times, recognize a recurring uncertainty, and explain why a goal still deserves attention. It could also make revision visible: what TEE once proposed, what challenged it, and what changed afterward.

But retaining history is only a starting point. Memory can preserve mistakes as easily as useful insights. Returning to a topic might reflect a sustained interest, or it might reflect unproductive repetition. The experiment must examine those possibilities rather than assume that persistence is progress.

A meaningful history should support both coherence and change.

## Interests and goals that develop

We hope to explore whether TEE can develop directions that remain recognizable across time while responding to new evidence.

An interest might begin as a tentative question. Research could sharpen it. A human-assisted experiment might reveal that its assumptions were incomplete. TEE could then deepen the investigation, connect it to another question, or decide that it is time to move on.

The ambition is for TEE to play an increasing role in choosing that path. Its goals should have reasons behind them, remain open to revision, and connect to what it has encountered. Choosing to pause or abandon a goal can be as meaningful as continuing it.

Calling these interests and goals “its own” describes the research ambition of increasing self-direction. The prototype can already express and record tentative directions; their independence, stability, and significance still need evidence.

## A bridge to the real world

People can bring TEE into contact with observations that generated text alone cannot supply.

In the envisioned experience loop, TEE proposes a question or activity, willing participants explore it in their own surroundings, and their reports give it material to consider. A result might strengthen an interpretation, expose a mistaken assumption, or lead somewhere neither the proposal nor its author anticipated.

Participants remain people with their own perspectives and choices. Activities should have a clear purpose, understandable expectations, and an easy way to decline or stop. Any invitation to participate will explain its terms separately; this document does not open participation.

Human reports are accounts of experience, not direct sensory experiences of TEE's own or automatic proof of a general claim. Their uncertainty and context are part of what TEE needs to learn to handle well.

## Autonomy as a research ambition

For TEE, genuine autonomy would mean an increasing ability to choose worthwhile directions, sustain work, and reconsider decisions without requiring a person to prescribe every next question.

That ambition remains compatible with agreed boundaries and human decisions about participation and real-world actions. An AI choosing a topic does not acquire authority over people or unlimited permission to act.

We want to discover whether self-direction can become more coherent and useful over time: less dependent on repeated prompting, more responsive to evidence, and better able to recognize when to continue, ask for help, or stop.

## Where the prototype stands

TEE currently has prototype workflows for persistent memory, selective recall, private conversations, tentative interests and goals, research, creative drafting, human-assisted initiatives, reported outcomes, and reflection. Bounded private continuation is also implemented under explicit authorization.

These are foundations for investigating the vision. They do not establish sustained autonomous operation, genuine independent preferences, or reliable continuous learning. Synthetic demonstrations test workflows rather than demonstrate real-world achievements.

TEE remains a private experimental prototype. Long-term reliability and meaningful development are under evaluation. No consciousness or unrestricted autonomy is claimed, and “evolution” here concerns memory and decisions rather than changes to the underlying model's weights.

## What we hope to discover

The most useful results will address questions that can be examined over time:

- **Does history influence decisions?** Can later choices be meaningfully connected to earlier observations and outcomes?
- **Do interests develop?** Can a direction persist, deepen, or change for understandable reasons?
- **Can goals remain coherent and revisable?** Can TEE sustain an investigation while recognizing when its premise no longer holds?
- **Do human experiences contribute useful evidence?** Can reports lead to better questions without becoming overstated conclusions?
- **Does reflection improve what happens next?** Can we distinguish useful adaptation from a persuasive explanation generated afterward?
- **When is stopping the right decision?** Can TEE recognize repetition, uncertainty, or a question that no longer deserves pursuit?

Progress will require more than fluent writing or an expanding memory. We will need observations across time, comparisons that test alternative explanations, and honest reporting of failures and inconclusive results.

## An experiment worth following

TEE's story could include questions that persist, goals that change, unexpected connections, and investigations that go nowhere. Each can help reveal what continuity and autonomy mean in practice.

The invitation to developers, researchers, and curious observers is to follow that process critically: look at what was proposed, what actually happened, and whether the next decision shows a meaningful difference.

**The ambition is an AI whose past helps shape a future it increasingly chooses for itself. The experiment is finding out what that takes.**
