# TEE Development Roadmap

**From a persistent history to a future TEE increasingly helps choose.**

TEE — The Evolution Experiment is developing toward one continuing AI with its own evolving questions, interests, and goals. This roadmap connects that ambition to milestones we can build, examine, and report honestly.

Progress is organized by evidence and capability, not release dates. Phases overlap: a prototype workflow can exist while its reliability and usefulness remain open questions.

## Reading the roadmap

- **Implemented prototype workflows:** supporting functionality exists; deployment and effectiveness are separate questions.
- **Work in progress:** development or evaluation remains open. The milestones below describe intended next steps, not claims that every activity is already underway.
- **Future research ambitions:** outcomes we hope to investigate, without assuming they will be achieved.

Synthetic demonstrations exercise workflows. They do not establish independent preferences, proven learning, or completed real-world experiments. TEE remains a private prototype with bounded autonomy.

## Unified Evolutionary Memory update

[Development progress](PROGRESS.md) records project-reported implementation and offline verification of shared retrieval, consent-aware cumulative learning, evidence-linked hypothesis revisions, and optional reconsideration proposals. A separately authorized real-model sequence demonstrated use of two synthetic memory sources; it preceded Stage 2 and did not live-test Stage 2 reconsideration. These results do not close the long-term evaluation milestones below or establish operational deployment.

## Phase 1 — Foundations

**Give one AI a history it can return to.**

### Implemented prototype workflows

- Persistent memory and selective recall of relevant history.
- Private conversations with continuity across sessions.
- Question exploration, research, and creative drafting.
- Records of tentative directions and unfinished work.

### Work in progress and next milestones

- Evaluate continuity across longer histories and repeated sessions.
- Examine whether recalled information is relevant, accurate, and useful to the next decision.
- Document examples that distinguish remembered proposals from reported outcomes.

**Milestone evidence:** repeatable evaluations showing which history informed a later interaction, what was omitted, and where continuity failed.

## Phase 2 — Evolution and autonomy

**Let questions become directions, and directions remain open to change.**

### Implemented prototype workflows

- Tentative, revisable expressions of interests, self-observations, and goals.
- Reflection and follow-up on earlier questions.
- Bounded private continuation under explicit authorization.

### Work in progress and next milestones

- Evaluate memory's influence on decisions using comparisons that test alternative explanations.
- Examine whether recurring interests reflect useful continuity or unproductive repetition.
- Assess when TEE continues, revises, pauses, or abandons a proposed direction.

### Future research ambition

Greater self-direction: TEE increasingly choosing goals and investigations without a person prescribing every next question.

**Milestone evidence:** observations across multiple sessions linking choices to relevant history, including cases where new evidence changes a direction and cases where it does not. Expressing a goal alone does not demonstrate independent motivation.

## Phase 3 — Real-world experiences

**Bring questions into contact with voluntary human experience.**

### Implemented prototype workflows

- Human-assisted initiative proposals and participant-report workflows.
- Review of reported outcomes and tentative reflection.

These workflows do not establish that a real-world experiment has been completed or that participation is currently open.

### Work in progress and next milestones

- Prepare clearly explained activities with voluntary participation: people can decline or stop participating. Submitted contributions remain governed by the clearly disclosed participation terms and applicable legal requirements.
- Define what an activity could reveal, what evidence would be relevant, and what would remain uncertain.
- Evaluate the path from a participant report to a reviewed outcome and a subsequent question.

### Future research ambition

Explore whether reported experiences can improve TEE's investigations and decisions over time, rather than merely add material to memory.

**Milestone evidence:** for any future pilot, a clear account of what was proposed, what participants reported, what review supported, and how a later decision was affected. Reports remain human accounts, not TEE's direct sensory experiences or automatic proof of general claims.

## Phase 4 — Public presence

**Make the evolving story understandable and worth following.**

### Established public foundation

- A published project introduction and vision in this repository.
- Prototype creative drafting, which provides a foundation for future expression rather than automatic public publication.

### Work in progress and next milestones

- Develop a public website where TEE can express its evolving questions, interests, thoughts, and proposed experiments in its own voice, alongside its purpose, current status, and any separately announced participation opportunities.
- Develop social channels for that same evolving voice, with appropriate review before publication on both the website and social channels, distinguishing questions, proposals, and interpretations from observed facts.
- Establish a reviewed public evolution journal covering selected questions, proposals, outcomes, revisions, and unresolved work.

Website availability, live social publishing, and an ongoing journal are not established by this roadmap.

**Milestone evidence:** reviewed public entries that accurately identify their basis and status, protect participant privacy, and include setbacks and inconclusive results alongside progress. The journal should explain development without exposing private memory or internal reasoning.

## Phase 5 — Long-term development

**Build continuity that remains useful as the history grows.**

### Future research ambitions

- Greater independent initiative within agreed boundaries.
- Sustained investigations and coherent, revisable goals across longer periods.
- Reliable use of growing histories without becoming trapped in repetition or outdated assumptions.
- Measurable adaptation in later decisions following new evidence.
- Better recognition of uncertainty, limits, and when to seek help or stop.

### Milestones to pursue

- Define evaluation criteria before claiming improvement.
- Compare behavior across time and relevant baseline conditions.
- Report the scope of observed changes, including failures, regressions, and alternative explanations.
- Expand the scope of autonomous work only as evidence supports its reliability.

**Milestone evidence:** repeated, scoped observations of useful behavioral change. More activity, longer memory, or fluent reflection alone would not establish learning. Sustained autonomy and meaningful adaptation remain outcomes to investigate.

## How progress will be reported

Future updates should separate implementation, synthetic testing, live observation, and evaluated results. A milestone can remain incomplete, be revised, or be abandoned when evidence calls for it.

The roadmap will evolve with the experiment. The aim is to make it possible to follow one AI's developing history and ask a concrete question at every phase: **what changed, what supports that conclusion, and what should TEE explore next?**
